# Chapter Eight: The Thirty-Day Plan

## The idea

None of this happens by itself. Knowledge without a schedule stays knowledge — and the person who reads a book and drifts back after a week isn't weak, he just has no **clear starting point**.

So: thirty days, fifteen minutes a day, one week per theme. It doesn't have to be perfect and you don't have to do everything. You have to do **one thing a day** — that's it.

## Plan rules (read this first)

1. **Fifteen minutes daily.** No more. On a day with extra time, rest — you're building a system, not burning yourself out.
2. **Never miss two days in a row.** One missed day is a coincidence. Two is a new habit called stopping. Miss a day, return the next one even for five minutes.
3. **80% is enough.** This isn't an exam. Twenty out of thirty days is a success.
4. **Don't restart from the beginning.** If you fall off, come back from **today**, not day one. Restarting from zero is the most famous way to fail.
5. **Write it down.** Every exercise gets written. What's in your head is an intention, not a plan.

---

## Week one: Starting

**Goal:** start small things, and break down one big thing.

**Daily practice (15 min):**
- Two minutes of "the silly start" on anything.
- One line on paper: "today's hardest thing, and its first piece of gravel".

**Weekly tasks:**
- **Day 1:** mountain map on your biggest task (three rocks + four gravel).
- **Day 2:** do the first gravel — even five minutes.
- **Day 3:** write the start card: "first step / first three minutes / failure plan".
- **Day 4:** try "just sit, no commitment" three times today.
- **Day 5:** pick something small you've postponed for a week and finish it in ten minutes.
- **Day 6:** log: when did starting come easily? when did you hit resistance?
- **Day 7:** review the week in five minutes; write one thing that worked.

---

## Week two: Environment

**Goal:** take the daily battle out of your way.

**Daily practice:**
- The sheet on the desk before you sleep (one line for tomorrow).
- The phone is not the first thing after waking.

**Weekly tasks:**
- **Day 8:** the three-box raid on your desk (15 minutes).
- **Day 9:** empty first screen + distracting apps in a folder inside a folder.
- **Day 10:** all notifications off except calls.
- **Day 11:** choose a **place signal** (lamp, playlist, cup) and use it every time you sit to work.
- **Day 12:** announce to the house: "I'm working from X to Y".
- **Day 13:** apply the 20-second rule to one thing (less friction on good, more on bad).
- **Day 14:** review: what's still pulling you? Make one fix only.

---

## Week three: Energy

**Goal:** know when your mind works, and put the right task at the right hour.

**Daily practice:**
- Three lines at night: best hour / worst hour / tomorrow's task in the sharp hour.
- At least one wave: 45 minutes on, 10 off, no screen.

**Weekly tasks:**
- **Day 15:** start logging energy (no changes yet).
- **Day 16:** keep logging.
- **Day 17:** keep logging, predict your window.
- **Day 18:** book the peak window for your hardest task — phone locked out.
- **Day 19:** choose a fixed wake time and start it tomorrow.
- **Day 20:** ten minutes of morning light + charger outside the bedroom.
- **Day 21:** review: did the window hold? adjust the schedule to it.

---

## Week four: System and boundaries

**Goal:** move the load out of your head, and protect what you built.

**Daily practice:**
- Three only per day (important / quick / backup).
- A transition card whenever you step away.

**Weekly tasks:**
- **Day 22:** one inbox — move everything in your head there.
- **Day 23:** set a ten-minute review alarm at the same time daily.
- **Day 24:** try visible time (a timer) on one task.
- **Day 25:** write three refusal sentences and use one.
- **Day 26:** set two windows for replying to messages.
- **Day 27:** first and last thirty minutes with no screen.
- **Day 28:** tell one person you're working on this.
- **Day 29:** review the month: what got easier?
- **Day 30:** write **your system** on one page — five things only that you'll keep.

## Failure rules (the heart of this chapter)

This plan **will** break. Not maybe — it's the nature of any thirty days. The difference between people who gain something and people who drift back isn't that the first group never broke — it's that they knew how to return.

**1) A missed day isn't failure; two are.** Don't let one day become an announcement of stopping.

**2) Don't change the plan the first time it feels hard.** Difficulty in the first four days is normal. Fast switching is the enemy.

**3) Go back to the simplest version.** Fifteen minutes too much? Make it five. Three tasks too many? Make it one. The small version you do beats the big version you imagine.

**4) Write instead of judging.** When you fail, write one line: "what happened?" Blame closes thinking; writing opens it.

## How do you know it's working? (three indicators)

You won't measure your whole life. Measure three things once a week:
1. **Starts:** how many times you began something within five minutes of thinking about it.
2. **Return:** after being interrupted, how fast did you come back?
3. **Calm:** the share of days you closed without feeling something left circling in your head.

If all three improved even slightly, the plan works. You don't need to feel like a new person. You need to feel that a **system** is running.

---

## Closing

There's no moment where you become "organised". There are days that flow, and days you sit on the bed unable to move. Both are normal, and both will keep happening.

What changes isn't that you stop facing this. What changes is that you have **tools** when it happens: one piece of gravel instead of a mountain, two minutes instead of a full plan, one sheet on the desk instead of fifteen things in your head, and one sentence for when someone cuts in.

Your system doesn't need to be beautiful. It needs to be **simple enough to use while tired** — because that's the moment you need it most.

Start small. Return every time. That's the whole story.
