# Chapter Seven: The Stories

Six rungs on a ladder. Six questions you answered. And every rung you climbed, you discovered there was another above it. And the higher you went... the bigger the question.

But there's something you noticed throughout the book: every rung had a story. Tarek, Sami, Dr. Heba, Sherif, Heba, Karim. Each of them was a witness to a rung. And every story confirmed one idea: this ladder isn't theory. This ladder is people. People like you. People with the same problems, the same fears, the same moments where you sit alone in silence.

These stories aren't exceptions. They're the rule. And every one of us — if we look honestly — will find ourselves on one of the rungs. Maybe on one. Maybe on two. Maybe at the end. Maybe still at the beginning.

In this chapter — I'll tell you six stories. Five of them are real people, each a witness to a rung. And the sixth story... is yours.

## Story One: Tarek and the Borrowed Destination (I Determined)

Tarek — whom we met in Chapter One — a general manager at a large company. On paper, successful. But from inside, Tarek was walking someone else's road. His father wanted him in management. His family was proud of "our son the manager." People around him saw management as success. And Tarek... took all that as his destination. Not because he chose it. Because he let it be chosen for him.

Until the day he stood in front of the mirror and asked: "Where am I going?" And the answer was shocking: "I'm not going anywhere. I'm walking someone else's road." Tarek wasn't a failure. Tarek was lost. And the difference between them is big: the failure knows where he's going and doesn't arrive. The lost one doesn't know where he's going in the first place.

Tarek didn't quit his job immediately. But he started making small decisions: to study what he wanted, to tell his father "thank you, but I'm going somewhere else." And in a year — Tarek was on his road. Not someone else's. His own.

**The rung:** I determined. The first rung. Everything starts with a question: "Where am I going?" And if the answer isn't yours — the whole ladder is shaky.

## Story Two: Sami and the Success That Cost Him Everything (I Arrived)

Sami — whom we met in Chapter Two — owner of a successful agency on paper. Revenue up, team growing, office, clients. But Sami lost. Lost his health. Lost his sleep. Lost his relationships. Lost his years to work. He reached "success" but that success was at the expense of everything else.

When Sami stopped and weighed his life — he discovered the scale was tipped. The work side was heavy. The life side... light. And success that costs you your health isn't success. It's depletion. And the difference: success enlarges you. Depletion shrinks you.

Sami didn't quit his work. But he started rebalancing the scale. An extra hour of sleep. A day a week with family. And every degree he restored — the success started having a taste. Not just the taste of exhaustion. The taste of meaning.

**The rung:** I arrived. What did I actually reach? Not just "I arrived" — "What did I arrive at, exactly?" And if you don't ask — you might arrive and discover you arrived somewhere you never wanted.

## Story Three: Dr. Heba and Impostor Syndrome (I Evaluated and Believed)

Dr. Heba — professor, researcher, department head — and every time she received an award, a voice inside said: "You're an impostor." Impostor Syndrome. The disease of the successful. And the bigger the success, the bigger the voice.

Dr. Heba evaluated her achievement every day — counted it, calculated it, published it. But from inside, she didn't believe it. She was like a bank with a full vault... but convinced the money was counterfeit. And the cure isn't "make sure you're smart." The cure: accumulate evidence. Every small proof — write it down. After a few months — look at the page and see: this isn't coincidence. This is me.

Dr. Heba is still recovering. But the voice that used to scream... became a whisper. And the whisper grows quieter every time she proves another degree. And belief in value — isn't a decision. It's an observation. And you observe every day.

**The rung:** I evaluated and believed. What's its value? And do I believe in it? And the difference between the two questions is the difference between inventory and ownership.

## Story Four: Sherif and the Notebook in the Drawer (I Benefited)

Sherif — bank branch manager — has a notebook with fifteen years of experience. A golden notebook. But it's in the drawer. And the drawer is locked. Sherif has the power... but doesn't use it. Like money under the floor tile: present, counted, but dead.

Until Tarek — a new employee — asked him to teach. And Sherif started opening the notebook. And every time he opened it, he discovered he knew more than he remembered. And that what he learned in fifteen years could be transferred in months. For the first time, Sherif felt he was eating from the money under the tile. Not just storing it. Spending it. Investing it.

**The rung:** I benefited. What you have and don't use... isn't value. It's a notebook in a drawer. And benefit is a decision — the decision to make what you have work.

## Story Five: Heba and the Steering Wheel (I Led)

Heba — the designer who has everything: skill, clients, experience. But every time a decision comes... she asks five people. And each opinion she takes as a decision. And the car turns in the direction of the last person who spoke. Not in her direction.

Until she saw Dina — younger, less skilled — but with her own studio. Because Dina held the wheel. And Heba... sat in the back. And then she understood: power doesn't drive. Leadership is what makes power move in a direction. Without it — power does one thing: it stays present.

Heba started small. One decision a week. "I decide." After a few weeks — her hands grew strong on the wheel. And big decisions became easier. Because her hands were used to it.

**The rung:** I led. The car moves whether you're driving or not. The difference: where you arrive.

## Story Six: You

Yes. You. This story isn't a tale. This story is a mirror. And I want you to look in it.

Where are you right now on the ladder?

- **I determined?** — Do you know where you're going? Or still asking?
- **I arrived?** — What did you actually reach? Or still counting steps without looking at the result?
- **I evaluated and believed?** — Do you believe in the value of what you've done? Or still saying "luck"?
- **I benefited?** — Are you using what you have? Or is it in the drawer?
- **I led?** — Are your hands on the wheel? Or is someone else driving?
- **I benefited others?** — Has what you learned reached someone else? Or will it die with you?

Don't answer me. Answer yourself. Honestly. Because this question — doesn't want a nice answer. It wants an honest answer. And the honest answer is what starts change. The nice answer... is what delays you.

## But My Circumstances Are Harder

I know. And I'm certain. Your circumstances are harder than Tarek's, Sami's, Dr. Heba's, Sherif's, Heba's, and Karim's. Maybe there's no money. Maybe your family takes every decision. Maybe your health doesn't cooperate. Maybe there's no time. Maybe your environment doesn't support.

I know. And I'm not here to say "be patient" or "stay positive." I'm here to tell you one thing:

This ladder isn't for the comfortable. This ladder is for the people whose circumstances are harder. Because the one whose circumstances are easy — doesn't need the ladder. They're walking and everything works. The one who needs the ladder... is the one who's stuck. The one who needs to determine their destination... is the one who's lost. The one who needs to evaluate what they have... is the one people diminish what they have. The one who needs to lead... is the one whose life everyone else drives.

Your circumstances are harder? Then this ladder is for you more than anyone else. Not less. Every rung we talked about — the people in those stories didn't have easy circumstances. Tarek was lost. Sami was depleted. Dr. Heba was an impostor in her own eyes. Sherif was frozen. Heba sat in the back. Karim... didn't transfer.

None of them were comfortable. Each was facing something. And each... climbed a rung. Not because circumstances helped. Because they decided. And that decision — doesn't need money. Doesn't need time. Doesn't need anyone. It needs you. Only.

And the ladder is yours. Start where you are. From the rung you're on. Not necessarily from the beginning. Maybe you've reached rung three. Maybe you've reached five. Start wherever you are. And climb.

## Before We Close the Chapter...

Six stories. Six rungs. Six questions. And all lead to one big question — the question that birthed this whole book: "What do I gain from what I reached?" And the answer... isn't in a word. The answer is in a journey. From first question to last. From first rung to last. From first day to last.

And the next chapter — the last — isn't a new rung. It's a pause. A pause above the whole ladder. You look down — see where you came from. You look up — see where you're going. You look ahead — see what remains. And the next chapter... the ending. And still not the end.
