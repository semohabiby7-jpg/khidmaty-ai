# Chapter Seven: Stories of Part Two

There's something words fall short of and a story does with ease: **words get locked in the mind... a story stays running.**

That's why Part One closed with a stories chapter, and this part will close with one like it. The stories coming are from real life — the names have been changed, and the details arranged, but the ladder they're walking is the same ladder you've been walking with me from the start of the book: **destination → decision → watering → storms → people → impact.**

Five stories. Each one shows you a rung of the ladder as it works in a real life.

## Nabeela's Story: Destination at 48

Nabeela is a commerce graduate, class of '99. From the day she married she was a housewife — twenty full years, three children who grew up and got educated. And on an ordinary day, while tidying the kitchen cabinet, she asked herself the question that hadn't been asked for twenty years: «Where am I going?»

She tried to answer with the official answer: «my children.» But she heard in her head this part's question: «and after them?»

And Nabeela's destination page turned into something simple: «a year from today: a computer accounting diploma... and a half-time job.» The people around her said the expected sentence: «at your daughters' age? meaning if it happens, a diploma won't come in a full year?» And Nabeela answered with the truth from the first chapter without having read it: **«This year will pass anyway, whether I'm like this or not... my only question is: where will it pass?»**

A year later: Nabeela is a half-time accountant in a clinic, first salary in her hand... the first time in twenty years she feels «this is mine.» And the rung the story shows you: **destination never ages... no matter how old you are when you ask.**

## Youssef's Story: The Night of Decision and the Day of Drought

Youssef is twenty-four, working and enrolled in an evening course. His old problem: 8 p.m. he opens the phone... and stays up till two. And the course gets taken in vain.

Youssef didn't try to «organize his time» — he tried that for years and you know the result. Youssef did one thing: a night of decision. He sat, and calculated the standing bill in numbers: a full year on the same system = **700 hours of staying up** and zero courses. And he wrote the decision sentence in the closed form, and signed: «I get up at 6. I won't try... I get up.»

Day nine was easy. Day twelve... the day of drought came exactly as we agreed: a crush at work, his mind drifted, and an inner voice: «rest today only. It doesn't make a difference.»

What saved Youssef was one thing: **the page.** Not enthusiasm — the page that had the 700-hour bill on it, hanging next to the mirror. He looked at it for ten seconds... and got up at 6:10. Not 6 sharp — 6:10. The smallest degree... and he carried on without opening the «why?» file again. Youssef finished the course today in five months... and is studying for a second certificate.

The rung: **the decision isn't built on enthusiasm... it's forged from the page. And the page wins the day of drought.**

## Soad's Story: Watering of 14 Months

Soad is thirty-five, an administrative employee, and has a hand — God bless it — in the kitchen. The dream: a home baking project from the house — half her income from her own oven.

The first five months were the expected disaster: three orders a month... twice from her family. And month six and seven and eight: the gap. The street is full of people like her, the feed is full of better than her, and the daily voice: «no use.»

Soad could have done one of two things: close the oven... or open every day the order number and wait for the miracle. She did a third thing — she made a **watering page**: every day one hour only, divided: developing a recipe, a photography trial, an online page. And she put the forbidden line on the page: «the harvest is forbidden from the count before 90 days.»

Month ten: the first full Eid order — **4,800 pounds** in one week. Not wealth... that's proof of direction. Today, after four years, this kitchen has become Soad's main work. And the rung: **the harvest never comes on its time... it comes when it finds sustained watering.**

## Uncle Ramadan's Story: The Storm and the People

Uncle Ramadan is fifty-five. A doorman of a building in downtown for thirty years. The storm wasn't a deduction or a failure — the storm was his wife's operation, and a full month between home and hospital, while he himself is a diabetic and blood pressure patient.

When he went back to work, he tried to «return complete» like anyone: from the first day full energy and full cleaning. Five days... and his leg gave out on him. And he stopped: if he kept on like this he'd fall... and no one would catch him.

Uncle Ramadan — without reading the storm chapter — made a smallest-degree rule by his hand: first week, he opens the gate and arranges the doorman's table only... and then sits. Second week: the gate and the greetings to the residents. Third: full work. A return at the smallest degree — without breaking the leg.

And the story has the second half of the ladder: **the people.** The older lady on the third floor — a retired doctor — brought him the diabetes and blood pressure medicines she takes herself: «no need to call me needed.» And the building's children started carrying furniture with him. And the engineer on the fifth floor, when he learned the story, said one sentence to him: «if you need anything... I'm on the fifth floor. Just knock.»

The rung: **the return is degree by degree... and who's around you is measured at storm time, not at ease time.**

## Mariam's Story: Two Hours

Mariam is nineteen, a student in a theoretical college, and one of the people most keen to revise with her friends before exams. One time she noticed something: while explaining to her friend, she studies better than by herself.

Instead of letting something sweet die in a cafeteria seat, she did something simple: a revision session every Thursday, two hours, in the empty lecture hall — for free, for anyone in the class who wanted to come. First Thursday: four. Second month: thirteen. The year after: forty students, and four from her class became teaching with her.

When they asked her why she does this when she herself has exams — she answered with one sentence that summarizes the «Whose service» chapter without knowing it: «I'm very happy and I don't know how to say why.»

The rung: **two sustained hours... build something bigger than their owner.**

## The Whole Ladder in Five Stories

- **Nabeela** → destination: the right question reaches you even if it was twenty years late.
- **Youssef** → decision: the page saves you on the day of drought... and enthusiasm doesn't.
- **Soad** → watering: the harvest comes after you leave its count and work on your watering.
- **Uncle Ramadan** → storm and people: the return is degree by degree... and people show their colours at the time of trouble.
- **Mariam** → impact: two hours a week become something bigger than you.

And notice one last thing — perhaps the most important thing in this chapter: **not one of them was without a fall.** Nabeela missed three and a half months of watering in her first year... and came back a degree. Youssef fell on day twelve. Uncle Ramadan fell literally. And the only difference between them and the ones who stand in the same place for years: **the degree of return.** Falling isn't the difference... returning is the difference.

## Chapter Exercise: Your Place in the Stories

This exercise is light — but it's in the book for a reason: so these stories keep their place with you.

1. **Go back to the five stories and choose the closest to your life.** Not the one you love... the closest. The first story that made you feel «this is me» — that's yours.
2. **Locate your place on the ladder:** where are you standing right now? Destination? Decision? Watering? Storm? People? Impact?
3. **Write the first step on your missing rung.** One step — and its smallest degree is ready and waiting in its chapter.

And four last traps:

- **«That's luck»:** notice the order: luck in these stories came after the decision and the watering... it never came before them.
- **«Their circumstances are better than mine»:** review Uncle Ramadan's story before this sentence.
- **«I'm different from them»:** right — the ladder doesn't need to look like you... it just needs you to walk on it.
- **«These stories scare, they don't encourage»:** it means you're looking at the distance between you and them. Look only at the first rung... and the rest comes one after another.

**The mark of success:** a story from the five stayed with you after you closed the book... and you started telling it to yourself while you work. The story you carry with you — that's the compass of your place on the ladder.

## Sentence of the Week

«**They started... doesn't mean they're different from you.**»

Write it and put it in front of you. All week, every time you find yourself saying about any successful person «those are other people» — remember that every one of these stories used to say the same sentence... before the first rung. And the difference between «other people» and you: one rung. That's all.
