# Chapter Five: Who's Around You?

In the chapters past we worked on the world from the inside: the destination, the decision, the patience, the storms. This chapter is about the last secret of the story — and the first secret in it:

**You never cross this road alone.**

You've judged your case from your mind and your system... but there's something stronger than both, working in the background without ever asking your permission: **the people around you.**

We'll begin the way we always begin... with a story.

## Omar's Story, and the Thursday Gathering

Omar is twenty-seven. A civil engineer at a consultancy. And he has a clear dream like the destination page we've been talking about since the first chapter: a new skill by night — web development — that flips him an income after a year. His system: six hours of learning a week, at night, after work.

And there's one other thing in his life as old as the dream: **the Thursday gathering.** The high-school crowd — every Thursday from nine at night to two in the morning. A genuinely good gathering: football, cards, memories. Omar loves them and they love him — and that's the most important sentence in this chapter, so hold it in your awareness.

The problem is the gathering had an opinion about the dream. Every time Omar opened the topic: "Man! You think you're Zuckerberg?" and a general laugh, and the topic gets thrown behind the gathering. No one hates him... but something moves without anyone intending it.

When Omar counted it in numbers, he found something stunning:

- The gathering: five hours every Thursday × 52 weeks = **260 hours a year.**
- And the course he needs for his dream: **240 hours.**

The gathering he loves... eats his whole dream and has 20 hours left over.

The storm — as we agreed in the last chapter, comes without asking permission — came this time as a layoff at the firm. Omar left the job in one day. The gathering gave its opinion the same day in the group chat: "Rejoice, man! The whole system is corrupt. Come sit with us... what has a God gives him."

And on that same day exactly, a message came from a completely different kind of people. Khaled — an old friend, not from the crowd, who writes to him from time to time and asks about his road seriously. The message: "I heard the news. I'm taking on a project the whole of next month and I don't have a developer... the code isn't urgent. Come, I'll see you tomorrow morning."

Look at the difference: some people told him **"sit"**... and some told him **"come."**

Omar went back to the numbers again, but this time not on the gathering's hours — on the direction's budget. He made an arrangement decision, not a cut: the gathering became once a month, not every Thursday. And the six learning hours got extended. And Khaled became a weekly sitting. And after a year... Omar's first real client for his project came from these night projects.

## What People Do to You Without You Feeling It

### First: You're the average of the five people you spend your time with — a tally, not wisdom

Your mind is a simulation device: it learns by observation before experience. One guy complains in every gathering? After a month you're complaining without a clear reason. Someone sees the world as bleak? Your feeling gets adjusted without you taking a decision.

And that's not emotional talk — it's a tally: the five people who take your time are five private tutors you're not paying money... you're paying them time. And time is what fattens them: those who sit with you most... teach into you most.

### Second: Whoever pulls you doesn't hate you... they love an old version of you

The hardest truth in this chapter. Pulling rarely comes dressed as hatred — most who pull you **love you.** But they apply that love to an old version of you: you like them, your sitting is their place, your ambition is postponed like theirs. The moment you try to move, it looks to them like you're leaving them... so they work automatically on returning you to the comfortable spot.

Like the crabs in the bucket: one tries to climb out, and the rest pull him down — not because they hate him. Because if one gets out, then the rest have admitted that the bucket isn't a place to sit. **Pull is mostly fear... wearing the clothes of love.**

And this understanding doesn't exempt anyone from their effect — it helps you handle it without cruelty: you're not cutting hearts... you're adjusting doses.

### Third: The charger isn't the one who says "bravo"... it's the one who asks "where have you reached?"

A fine and dangerous difference. Empty praise rests you — and rest is the drainer's top wish. But the question by name — "did you finish the second part of the course?" — is something else entirely: someone is logging your arrival.

Marks of the real charger:

- Asks about your project **by name**... not about your general state.
- Tells you "I did such-and-such" — so he charges you with example, not advice.
- In storm time he moves with an act... not verbal consolation.
- After you sit with him, you feel you have **more energy**... not less.

And the final test: the charging gathering leaves fuel in you. The draining one draws from your battery without you noticing.

### Fourth: It's not a cut... it's a dose arrangement

The mind is a social creature — total isolation is a punishment, not a solution. And total immersion in those gatherings is your dream being eaten in instalments. The solution that actually works, in a clear half: **the ratios.**

Reduce the dose of the drainers... and increase the dose of the chargers. One charging gathering a week is stronger than five entertainment ones. And the golden rule: who doesn't choose their circle by hand... will have their days arranged for them without them feeling it.

## Chapter Exercise: The Five Tally

**What you need:** a page, a quiet hour, and a dose of honesty with yourself — because this exercise opens doors that deserve opening.

1. **Write the five people who take the biggest time from your week.** Not the people you love most... the people most present in your life. Honesty here is hard and required.
2. **Estimate the hours:** next to each one, write the weekly hours approximately.
3. **Mark next to each one:** charger / drainer / neutral — by the marks of the chapter above, not by the momentary feeling.
4. **Write the chargers list.** If only one name ends up on it — that's fine, and start looking for another: a professional group, someone on a road you like, one monthly gathering.
5. **The dose decision:** for the drainers — no sudden cut. One decision only: reduce the appointments or reduce the duration. (Omar reduced the appointments from four a month to one.)
6. **Add a new charger this month:** a message, a gathering, a group subscription — anything that adds a dose of fuel.

And four common traps:

- **"They're my friends from way back":** Duration isn't a measure... direction is the measure. The only question: after the gathering, are you charged or tired?
- **"I'm strong, it doesn't affect me":** Social habit is stronger than any individual will — and your mind imitates while asleep.
- **"I'll cut them all and continue alone":** An emotional reaction — after two weeks it relapses worse, and loses the innocent neutrals with it.
- **"I can't find chargers around me":** The charger doesn't have to be close: a channel that charges, a book, a group with your direction. The important thing is that the phenomenon you sit with... charges.

**The mark of success:** After the first month of arranging the doses, look at your energy budget: you sit with people and rise feeling you've **grown a bit**... not shrunk. That's the only indicator.

## Sentence of the Week

"**I sat with them... doesn't mean I'm going with them.**"

Write it and put it in front of you. All week, in every gathering, ask yourself the first chapter's question anew: "Where's this gathering going?" If it's not in the direction of your destination — that's fine, take your dose and walk on... but take the dose by your own hand, not by the order of appointments.

## Before We Close the Chapter...

Omar now, a year in: the gathering is still there — once a month, they joke and laugh exactly like before. But the difference became clear to him alone: the gathering has become a rest station... not an arrival station. The moment he rises, he continues his road.

Up to this point in the book we've worked on the best version of you: a clear destination, a closed decision, watering patience, and a return at the smallest degree, and arranging the people around you. But one last question remains — and the most beautiful of them all:

This power... **whom will it serve?**

Because there's a dangerous difference between someone strong... and someone strong with a reason to exist. And the next chapter is about the moment Power of Mind crosses your own limits — when what you planted in yourself starts to bear fruit in other people's lives. The next chapter is the spirit of *Khidmaty* all of it... and it's the part the whole book was made for:

**"Your Power, in Service of Whom?"**
