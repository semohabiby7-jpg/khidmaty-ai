Chapter One: I Was Driven

Every one of us has a driver's seat. A seat where the one sitting decides the direction, the speed, and when to stop. And every one of us has someone sitting in it — sometimes you, sometimes something else. The problem is: you don't feel the switch.

Ahmed and the Seat He Didn't Know Was Empty

Ahmed is thirty-two. An accountant. Good at his job, respected by his colleagues, and — in his own quiet estimation — underpaid. For two years, he's been thinking about opening a small accounting office. Not a company, not a firm — just a small office. He has the experience, he has the license, and he has a network of small clients who'd follow him. Everything is ready. Everything except one thing: he hasn't moved.

Ahmed didn't sit still because he was lazy. He sat still because every time he got close to the first step, something inside him pulled back. Not a thought — a feeling. A tightness in his chest. A voice that said: "Not now. The timing isn't right. What if it doesn't work?" And every time the feeling came, Ahmed obeyed. Not because he decided to obey... because he didn't know he was obeying. He thought he was being cautious. He was being driven.

The feeling was fear. And fear — old, fast, powerful — was sitting in Ahmed's driver's seat. Not because it took it by force. Because Ahmed left it empty, and fear, like nature, abhors a vacuum. When you don't sit in your seat, something else will.

The problem isn't that emotions sit in the seat. Emotions will always sit there — they're fast, they're old, they get there first. The problem is: you don't know they're there. And what you don't know... you can't change.

I was driven. I didn't know. Now I do.
