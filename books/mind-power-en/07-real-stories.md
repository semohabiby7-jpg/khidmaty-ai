# Chapter Seven: Real Stories of People Who Changed Their Lives and the World with the Power of the Mind

We have finished six rules. And we agreed at the end of the last chapter that there is an objection coming, as repetitive as the rules themselves: "Nice words on paper... but the real world is harsher than that."

This chapter is the reply. Not with theory or philosophy... with names. Five true stories from five different places, and each story is a living witness to one rule of this book. Read them, and you be the judge.

And after them comes the sixth story... which will be different from the five before it.

## 1. Ahmed Zewail — A Mind from Desouq That Reached the Nobel
**Witness of: "Who Charges Whom"**

He was born in 1946 in Desouq, a small quiet city on the Nile. Circumstances of the kind people call "ordinary": a small city, and less than many. But the house he was born into had one different thing: questions were received. The curiosity of a child asking "why?" was not met with "be quiet"... it was met with seriousness. And that is not a small detail — in the accounting of chargers, it is a charge.

A dream without a title was planted in his head: science. And from that day he chose his environments one after another the way you choose healthy food: the university, the laboratory, the science city, the work team that speaks his language. And in a faraway lab, he worked on something hard for anyone even to imagine: photographing the moment of the chemical reaction itself — the fastest "camera" in history: the femtosecond, one part in a thousand million million of a second... faster than any flash.

In 1999, in Stockholm, the first Egyptian and Arab to take the Nobel in the sciences. From a small city on the Nile... to a moment shorter than any flash.

Zewail did not happen by chance — he was engineered by a charging environment. In his home... and afterwards, he chose it himself, one after another.

## 2. Jim Carrey — The Check Written Before the Millions
**Witness of: "Your Mind Believes What Gets Repeated"**

The year 1985. A young actor in Los Angeles, trying without results, his money stretched to the size of the dream. One night, he did something strange: he sat and wrote himself a check. The amount: ten million dollars. The line: "for acting services rendered." And the date: five years from that day. He fixed the check in his wallet, and decided to walk behind it.

Every night, he would take the car up a hill overlooking the city, and imagine in detail: the studio directors saying "We love you... welcome." This scene repeated hundreds of times... the way Salim's dark resignation images repeated — but in the other direction.

In 1994 — before the check's date — it came to pass that he would take ten million dollars for a single film. The exact amount. The check in his wallet... came true. (And later he placed it in his father's grave — his father, the first one who believed.)

And here we must stop for a moment of honesty: this story had no "wish and watch." He was going to acting auditions at the same time, working on his skill, and the repeated image was the bridge that let every loss pass without breaking him. Repetition does not cancel the work... repetition carries you to its door.

## 3. Oprah Winfrey — The Girl Who Changed the Story
**Witness of: "The Words You Say to Yourself"**

She was born in rural Mississippi, in dire poverty, in a childhood harsh by every measure childhood is measured by. The world was ready to tell her one broken story about herself: "You are a girl of your circumstances... the story ends at these borders."

She refused this story — not once, every day. Over years, she changed the person speaking in her head about her: from "what happened to me" to "what I will do." And she moved from the mirror that audits, to the mirror that thanks: a small nightly gratitude — five things from the day worth being thankful for.

The girl the world had written a ready-made story for... became one of the biggest names in the history of media, and her sentence is now repeated in millions of mouths: "You become what you believe."

The first thing Oprah changed was not her job or her look... it was the sentence she said to herself.

## 4. David Goggins — The Minute That Opened the Marathon
**Witness of: "The One-Minute Rule"**

In his twenties, he weighed almost three hundred pounds, had asthma, worked a job far from any dream, and said worse things about himself than all of that. One night, drained from his day, he watched a documentary about the elite units of the US Navy... and spent the rest of the night saying to himself: then why not me?

In the morning, he went out to run. The first walk nearly killed him — his body was distances away from sport. The beginning was smaller than small: one step after another, up to the first street. The next day... he went back. The day after... he went back. The only mission was: "return today as well" — the one-minute rule, walking on two legs.

The result after years: the US Navy's elite unit (their hardest training), superhuman marathons, and a world record in pull-ups: thousands of reps in a row. And the rule he still teaches people to this day is harder and simpler than all of it: "When your mind tells you it is over... you are only 40% done."

Goggins did not wait for his body to be ready or his enthusiasm to arrive. One day, he did one small thing... and after that, every day was the smallest decision of the day: return.

## 5. Stephen Hawking — The Fastest Mind in the Slowest Body
**Witness of: "Your Mind Is Not You"**

At twenty-one, a brilliant PhD student at Cambridge... came the diagnosis that stops stories: a disease that shuts down the nerve cells, and the traditional story says: "two years left."

The expected novel ended there. The real one began.

The body surrendered itself piece by piece: the legs, the hands, the voice. Hawking sat in the chair... and his mind became the one that moves. He reached places his body never reached: the edges of time, the borders of the black hole, and a small radiation that got named after him. And he wrote "A Brief History of Time" — a book of theoretical physics that sold millions of copies and entered the homes of people who cannot tell two equations apart. And he lived to 76... meaning 55 full years after the "two years." And his laugh was more famous than the laughs of far healthier men.

The disease imprisoned his body completely... and his mind never entered the prison. That is why Hawking is our first chapter, walking: you are not the body. Not even the circumstances. You are the one who sees.

## The Common Denominator

Look at the five together: one from a small city on the Nile. One who slept for years with a paper check in his wallet. One the world had written a ready-made broken story for. One whose brain told him "it's over" while he was at his first 40%. And one who left his body entirely... and his mind reached the edges of time.

Not one of them waited for ideal circumstances. Or permanent enthusiasm. Or a miracle coming down from the sky.

Three things only repeated across the five stories: **they saw**, like the Watcher. **they repeated**, like the program. **they began**, like the one minute. Everything else was details.

## Apply the Witnesses to Your Life

The stories are not for amazement... they are for use. Take your witness and take your beginning from him:

- **From Zewail — for the environment:** the first thing you control is not your talents... it is what surrounds you. A whole chapter was spent auditing your home for a reason — first step, tomorrow morning: remove one drainer from your day.
- **From Jim Carrey — for the programming:** where is the "check" in your wallet? One honest sentence repeated in front of you every day. First step: make your replacement sentence your phone wallpaper today.
- **From Oprah — for the words:** what is the ready-made story written about you? Log it, and edit it with an honest sentence. First step: write the first line of the true novel tonight.
- **From Goggins — for the beginning:** where is your "first street"? The smallest version of the postponed thing — one workout. First step: one minute of it before sleep tonight.
- **From Hawking — for the watching:** where is the "it's over" sentence in your head? See it as a train passing — and you are the one on the platform. First step: notice it next time, without boarding it.

Five steps... each one of them can be done today. And all five together? That is day one of the coming 21-day plan.

## 6. The Sixth Story: You

The sixth story has no name and no date... it is still being written.

You are the one who has read seven chapters up to this line. The one who now knows he has a platform to watch his thoughts from, a bill he pays for every day, an inner voice being edited, repeated images he chooses among, a minute that breaks the gravity, chargers being tuned... and living witnesses of flesh and blood saying that none of this is imagination.

The five previous stories are written and done, and they will not change. As for the sixth — its next passage is being written right now... in the coming lines of your own life.

And the beautiful thing about this story is one thing: you are not its narrator.
You are its author.

## "But My Circumstances Are Harder"

The objection in the head of one out of every two readers of these lines must be met with honesty: "Nice... but I am not Zewail or Oprah. My circumstances are harder."

The truth? Most of the five said this exact sentence at one point or another. Jim Carrey slept in a car on the point of falling apart — and said it. Oprah was born into dire poverty — and said it. Goggins had asthma and a body far from any athletic dream — and said it. Circumstances are not excuses... circumstances are the starting ground of every one of them.

And the difference between you and them is not in the circumstances... the difference is one small point: each of them stopped once and said: "Then I will tell my story too... with the tools I have right now." Not with dream tools. Not with anyone else's tools. And from there, the story began.

And one last thing honesty deserves: the five stories did not happen in a day — they took years. The first year in each of them looked like the first year of any story starting today: small steps, broken days, returns made with the smallest step. The only difference? They were seeing... even in the middle of the fog.
