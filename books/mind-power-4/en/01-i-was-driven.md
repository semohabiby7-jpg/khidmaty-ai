# Chapter One: I Was Driven

Every one of us has a driver's seat. A seat where the one sitting decides the direction, the speed, and when to stop. And every one of us has someone sitting in it — sometimes you, sometimes something else. The problem is: you don't feel the switch.

And like we always do... we'll start with a story.

## Ahmed and the Seat He Didn't Know Was Empty

Ahmed is thirty-two. An accountant. Good at his job, respected by his colleagues, and — in his own quiet estimation — underpaid. For two years, he's been thinking about opening a small accounting office. Not a company, not a firm — just a small office. He has the experience, he has the license, and he has a network of small clients who'd follow him. Everything is ready. Everything except one thing: he hasn't moved.

Ahmed didn't sit still because he was lazy. He sat still because every time he got close to the first step, something inside him pulled back. Not a thought — a feeling. A tightness in his chest. A voice that said: "Not now. The timing isn't right. What if it doesn't work? What if you lose what you have?" And every time the feeling came, Ahmed obeyed. Not because he decided to obey... because he didn't know he was obeying. He thought he was being cautious. He thought he was being smart. He was being driven.

The feeling was fear. And fear — old, fast, powerful — was sitting in Ahmed's driver's seat. Not because it took it by force. Because Ahmed left it empty, and fear, like nature, abhors a vacuum. When you don't sit in your seat, something else will.

Ahmed's breaking point came on a Tuesday. A colleague — younger, less experienced, with half the skill — opened an office. Not a big one. A small one. The kind Ahmed had been planning for two years. Ahmed saw the announcement on social media, and something inside him cracked. Not anger. Something deeper: regret. The taste of two years that won't come back. And in that moment, Ahmed asked himself a question he'd never asked before: "Why didn't I do it?"

The answer that came wasn't about money or time or the market. It was one word: fear. Fear was driving. And Ahmed was in the backseat, watching his life pass by through the window, thinking he was the one steering.

## The Driver's Seat

Here's the idea — simple, but it changes everything if you understand it:

You have a driver's seat. The one sitting in it runs your life. Not metaphorically — literally. When fear sits there, you retreat. When anger sits there, you attack. When excitement sits there, you rush. When frustration sits there, you stop. And you — the conscious you — think you're the one doing it. You're not. You're the passenger.

The passenger goes where the driver takes him. If the driver is fear — you go backward. If the driver is excitement — you go forward, but not necessarily where you want. If the driver is anger — you go into collision. And the passenger, looking through the window, thinks he chose the road.

The problem isn't that emotions sit in the seat. Emotions will always sit there — they're fast, they're old, they get there first. The problem is: you don't know they're there. And what you don't know... you can't change.

Ahmed didn't know fear was driving. He thought it was caution. He thought it was wisdom. It was fear wearing caution's clothes. And because he didn't know, he couldn't do the one thing that would have changed everything: sit down.

## The Idea in Points

### First: you have a seat, and it's not always occupied by you

This is the foundation of the whole book. Not that you have emotions — everyone knows that. That there's a seat, and the one sitting in it determines the direction of your life in any given moment. And that the seat changes hands — sometimes you, sometimes fear, sometimes anger, sometimes excitement — without you noticing.

When you don't notice, the switch happens in the dark. And in the dark, fear is faster than you. It reaches the seat before you even know the engine started.

### Second: the first step isn't "don't feel"... it's "know that you're feeling"

You can't fight what you don't see. And you can't see what you don't know is there. The first step — the one this whole chapter is about — isn't "stop being afraid." It's "know that you're afraid." Know that the tightness in your chest isn't caution... it's fear. Know that the retreat isn't strategy... it's emotion. Know that the one driving right now... isn't you.

This knowing doesn't make the fear disappear. It does something more important: it gives you a choice. Because you can't choose who drives if you don't know the seat is occupied.

### Third: the gap between knowing and doing is where the passenger lives

Ahmed knew the right thing. He knew the office was a good idea. He knew he had the experience. He knew the market was ready. Knowledge wasn't his problem. The gap — the distance between knowing and doing — that's where the passenger lives. And what fills that gap isn't more knowledge. It's who's in the seat.

### Fourth: you were driven — and that's not a flaw, it's a starting point

Being driven doesn't mean you're weak. It means you're human. Every person on earth is driven by something until they know they're driven. The difference between the driven and the driver isn't strength. It's awareness. And awareness — as we'll see in every chapter — is the first key.

## Chapter Exercise: The Driver's Seat

A simple exercise. Takes two minutes. Needs nothing but a pen and paper.

Think of one decision you've been delaying — not a big one, a small one. A message you haven't sent. A call you haven't made. A step you haven't taken. Now write:

1. **"What am I feeling when I think about this decision?"** (Write the feeling — fear, anxiety, hesitation, anything. Don't analyze it. Just name it.)
2. **"Who's in the driver's seat when I think about this?"** (Be honest. Is it you? Or is it the feeling?)
3. **"If I were the one driving — not the feeling — what would I do?"** (Write one action. One small step.)

That's it. The exercise isn't about taking the step — not yet. It's about seeing the seat. Seeing who's in it. Because what you see... you can change. And what you don't see... drives you.

## Weekly Sentence

**I was driven. I didn't know. Now I do.**

Live with this sentence for seven days. Every time you retreat, every time you delay, every time you feel something pulling you back — say it. Not as blame. As seeing. The seeing is the beginning of sitting.

## Before We Close the Chapter...

You discovered you were driven. You saw the seat. Now comes the next question: what are you being driven by? Not the general word — the specific name. Because fear isn't just fear. Anger isn't just anger. Each emotion has a face, a voice, and a direction. And in the next chapter, you'll learn to name them.

Because you can't manage what you can't name.
