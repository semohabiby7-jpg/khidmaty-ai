# Chapter 6: A Unit Doesn't Sell on Words — It Sells on Being Seen

## The problem

An ad is not copy. An ad is a **photograph**, and the copy only works once the photo stops the scroll.

Most agents do the same thing every time: shoot at midday, under ceiling lights, wide angle, upload nine near-identical photos — and then wonder why the ad gets views and no WhatsApp messages.

Two problems, and they're connected:

**One:** the photos say "empty unit" instead of "a possible life". Nobody buys square feet; they buy a picture in their head.

**Two:** the copy next to the photos — headline, description, ad — is written in the old formula, "apartment for sale", which attracts no one because everyone writes it.

## The method, part one: photo order — seven photos is enough

You don't need thirty photos. You need order. Order is what keeps the buyer scrolling forward:

| Order | Photo | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The facade or the view in the best light | Makes them ask "where is this?" |
| 2 | The widest angle of the living room | Conveys space |
| 3 | A close detail (kitchen / finishing) | Proves quality |
| 4 | The master bedroom | Their personal project |
| 5 | The bathroom | The photo buyers fear most |
| 6 | The balcony or the view from inside | Prevents questions, builds desire |
| 7 | The building or the street | Context and safety |

**The first-photo rule:** the first photo doesn't have to be the prettiest — it has to be the most **arresting**. That differs by unit: a view, a facade, a finishing detail. Test one photo at a time; change one thing per round.

**The light rule:** shoot at sunset or early morning. Yellow artificial light ruins any apartment, and a ceiling in the middle of the frame shrinks the space.

## The method, part two: three ad angles, not one

Every buyer moves for a different reason, so one ad for everyone is an ad for no one. Write three versions, one per angle:

| Angle | For whom | Opening line |
|---|---|---|
| **The solution** | The one with a problem | "Paying 15k in rent and can't save a down payment?" |
| **The comparison** | The one comparing | "Same area, same size, 300k apart — here's why" |
| **The life** | The one imagining | "Morning coffee on an open view, school four minutes away" |

All three describe **the same unit**, and each opens a different door. Don't judge them by opinion — if the platform gives you numbers, let the numbers decide.

## The ready prompt: a full ad package

> You are a real-estate copywriter in the Egyptian market, specialising in turning views into WhatsApp messages.
>
> **Unit data:** [type, size, location, price, down payment, monthly, top three features, best fit]
> **Photos:** [describe the photos in the order I plan to upload them]
>
> **Deliver:**
> 1. Reorder the photos for maximum selling power, explain the order in one line, and tell me if any photo is missing or weak.
> 2. Write three short ads (three lines each), each on a different angle: problem, comparison, life. Each ends with one clear WhatsApp call to action.
> 3. Write five very short headlines (six words max).
> 4. Write one long-form description for platforms that allow it (Facebook / website), with specs as bullets.
> 5. Suggest three questions I should answer inside the ad itself to cut down useless messages.
>
> **Banned:** hype, numbers not in the data, return promises, and the word "opportunity" in the first line.

### Before / after

**Before:** "Apartment for sale in a premium compound — 1,400 sq ft — fully finished — call for details."

**After (the life angle):** "Coffee on an open view, the school four minutes away, and the main road only when you feel like leaving. 1,400 sq ft, three bedrooms, move-in ready. Price 4.2M, down payment split over three payments. Send 'details' and I'll send you everything."

The second doesn't try to persuade — it gives a **scene**, then a number, then one easy step.

## Mistakes that waste the ad

| Mistake | Why it fails | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Nine near-identical photos | Suggests every room looks the same | Seven photos in a deliberate order |
| First photo is a ceiling or a wall | Nothing stops the scroll | View / facade / a strong detail |
| "Opportunity" and "call for details" | Ten-year-old language | Scene + number + one step |
| One ad for everyone | It's an ad for no one | Three angles |
| Hiding the price | People ask, then vanish | State the price or a range |
| Photos with no description | The tool can't see images | Describe the photos in the prompt |

## Chapter exercise (20 minutes)

1. Pick one unit and shoot seven photos in the order above.
2. Write the ad package with the prompt.
3. Run the three angles as three separate ads.
4. After 48 hours, check which angle produced real messages — not views.

## Chapter line

> The photo stops the buyer; the words make them message. If the photo doesn't stop them, no copy in the world can make up for it.
