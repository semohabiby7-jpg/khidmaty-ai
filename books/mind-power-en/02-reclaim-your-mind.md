# Chapter Two: Reclaim Your Mind

In the last chapter we agreed on two things: that your mind is a thought factory running without your permission... and that you are not these thoughts — you are the one who sees them. And that the Watcher in you is the most valuable thing you own.

But let me ask you the question we left hanging at the end of the last chapter: the trains of thoughts that stop at the platform of your mind... who is sending them? Who decides that the "worry about money" train arrives exactly while you are trying to sleep?

We will answer with another story. The story of a man named Karim... and you will find that it is your story too, and that it happens to you every day.

## Karim and the Hidden Bill

Karim wakes up at six in the morning. The first thing he does is not water, not brushing his teeth, not any of the things he dreamed of when he set the alarm. The first thing: reaching his hand to the phone on night mode next to the bed.

He opens the night's notifications: twenty-three of them. He replies to two. Laughs at a third video. Reads a piece of news that bores him. Remembers he has to send an email to a colleague at work. Gets up to drink water while still holding the phone... and the moment he lifts his eyes from the screen, it is 6:44.

The forty minutes that were supposed to go to prayer, breakfast, and an unhurried start... gone. None of them came back.

At work? Same story. Between one task and the next, a "quick" look at the phone. A look with no goal — a routine look. His hand pulls the phone before his mind makes any decision at all. He opens the app, watches, locks, goes back to work... four minutes each time. One time after another, the whole day.

But the moment that reached Karim... was not at work. It was at night, at home.

His nephew, seven years old, came running to him: "Uncle Karim! Come play football on the roof!"

Karim answered without lifting his eyes from the phone: "Just one second, sweetheart... let me finish this video."

The second turned into thirty-five minutes.

When Karim looked up, the boy was gone. Not upset, not crying, not saying anything... he walked away without taking an answer. Normal. He was used to it.

And for the first time in a long while, that scene pinched Karim's heart — a real pinch. But what matters is what happened after. Karim sat on the couch, opened the phone settings, the "Screen Time" section. The number: six hours a day. An average of six hours. Every single day.

He went to get a paper and pen and calculated, the way the engineer in him loves to:

Six hours a day × 365 days a year = 2,190 hours.
2,190 ÷ 24 = 91 full days.

Ninety-one days. Full days, twenty-four hours each. Not sleeping through them, not working through them, not living them with the people he loves... a screen.

And then he remembered a sentence a friend of his said at an old gathering — one he had laughed at back then and called "empty talk": "Karim, you are not working for free... you are working for them for free."

He had done the math right. Karim works for the app companies. A full workday: six hours a day, no salary, no insurance, no weekly day off... and nobody thanking him.

## The Idea in Points

### First: There is no such thing as free... the price is paid from your attention

Any "free" app you use — did you ever ask yourself: where does it pay from? This thing gets designed, gets developed, runs on servers that cost money, and its developers take salaries in the billions. And where does that money come from?

From ads. And an ad does not reach anything if nobody sees it. And the viewing only happens through one thing: that you stay inside the app for as long as possible. So the only thing the app is designed to take from you is... your attention. Your eyes. The minutes of your life.

The sentence that needs to be engraved in your mind from here to the end of the book: **if you are not paying for the product... then you are the product.**

No one in this world gives you something for free. In everything you take without money, there is a price paid from somewhere else: from your time, from your focus, from your data, from your heart while you watch. Money is a currency, true... but attention is your most expensive currency. Why? Because it is the only one that never comes back. The pound that went may return... the minute that passed never does.

### Second: Addiction is not an accident... it is a design

Pay close attention to this sentence, because it will acquit you of a charge you were never guilty of: **you are not weak. The system is designed.**

Behind every app that takes more of your time than you intended, there is a team of the most skilled engineers and psychologists in the world, working toward one goal measured by the minute: that you stay one more minute. Not a minute for their sake — a digital minute measured by a machine, and every single one of them is taken from your life.

The red badge that pops up over the app — designed to look "urgent" even if the content is trivial. The video that plays by itself — so that even the decision to "press play" is not left to you. The page with no end — so there is no "done" moment to tell your mind: get up.

And the most dangerous tool of them all: **the variable reward.** The exact same mechanism a slot machine runs on. You do not know the next result: the next video might make you laugh, might bore you, might be the most beautiful thing you saw all day. And that uncertainty is precisely what plays on the deepest instinct in your mind: "Pull one more time... maybe the next one is it." The slot machine tells itself the exact same words... and keeps spinning until it takes the last pound in the pocket.

So the moment you feel you will "just watch one thing for a second" and find yourself a quarter of an hour later — do not say "I am weak." What happened is not weak willpower... it is a complete design working against your will. And that is exactly why the solution is not "stronger willpower." The solution is that you see the game itself... and that is what we are doing right now.

### Third: Whoever does not see the cost... cannot choose

Here the law of the first chapter comes back to us: "Whoever does not see does not choose."

Hisham found himself riding a train without seeing it... ten minutes gone. Karim pays ninety-one days a year without seeing the bill. And both are in the same trap: there is no platform to watch from.

So the first step of the reclaiming is not throwing your phone into the sea and living in a cave. The first step is much simpler and much harder at the same time: **that you see the bill with your own eyes.**

Whoever measures, can. The moment the number is written in front of you — not a feeling, not an estimate, a number on paper — half the battle is over. Because the Watcher in you, the moment he sees the "ninety-one days" train parked on his platform... makes a decision. Not because he is angry at himself — because he now knows who is paying the price... and who is collecting it.

### Fourth: Your day is a budget... and you are the one spending it

Let us put the final frame on the picture: your day is one balance — about sixteen waking hours. That is all you have, and everything is spent from it: work takes from it, family takes from it, apps take from it, worry takes from it, and sleep takes the rest.

The difference is that your money is accounted for to the last penny: a hundred extra pounds on one bill gets a full hour of thinking, questioning, and reviewing from you. But the six daily hours you spend on a screen? They pass with no bill at all. And that is exactly the secret of the hidden bill: small expenses get audited... and massive ones pass in silence.

So use your accounting mind, but in the right direction: before you open any app — any app — one question only: "The bit I am spending from the balance... what is it bringing back?" If the answer is "a little laughter" — take your time, you are free and it is your right. But let it be clear information you have chosen... not a theft in the dark. That is the difference between the one who chooses to open... and the one who gets opened.

## Chapter Exercise: The Attention Audit

This exercise lasts one week, and it is done once on the first day — the rest is simple follow-up. Get a paper and pen... and open your phone settings:

1. **Pull up the screen time number for the last seven days.** The name: "Screen Time" on iPhone, and "Digital Wellbeing" on Android. Write the number on the paper in hours — no, not on the phone. On paper. Paper sticks.

2. **Look at the top three apps eating that number,** and write their names below the number in order.

3. **Do the yearly calculation:** the number ÷ 7 = daily hours. Daily hours × 365 ÷ 24 = how many full days a year are you giving to these apps? Write the final number on its own line... and read it out loud. This number is the price of your "free."

4. **Lock the notifications of all apps** except those where a real human sends something: calls, and real messages. The rest are not notifications — they are invitations to parties being held inside your head... and nobody said you have to attend.

5. **The first thirty minutes of your day without the phone, and the last thirty minutes before sleep without the phone.** The phone gets locked and placed away from the bed — outside the room if you can. This is the first piece of land you take back for your kingdom... and the most important land of all: the beginning of your day and its end.

6. **Every night before sleep: log the day's screen time number.** Seven simple marks. At the end of the seven, look at the line... and go ahead and ask yourself: what is this number bringing back?

**The sign of success:** it is not that the number drops to zero — we are not against the phone, and in a little while you will see that the phone itself can become a weapon in your hand. The sign of success is that the phone buzzes in your pocket... and a moment before you take it out, you find the Watcher in you asking: "This time... am I the one deciding to open, or the notification deciding for me?" If that question happens to you even once a day — the reclaiming has begun.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**"All my work is on the phone... lock what, exactly?"**
No one asked you to lock your work. The distinction is not between phone and no phone — it is between "I am the one who opened it" and "the notification opened me." Your work gets opened in your own time... and the notification gets opened in its time. And this exercise is the tool of that distinction, in numbers.

**"The numbers came out scary... I felt like a terrible person."**
The opposite: there is no crime here. The count is not a trial — this is the first time you are seeing a real bill. And the one who sees the bill pays less... and the one who does not see it pays in full, for life. Two minutes of feeling bad is fine... and after them, start from the smallest item.

**"I tried before, locked the notifications, and went back after two days."**
You went back because last time it was a total blackout — and everything that gets shut by force opens by force. This time it is much smaller: one single notification you let go of, and one single screen you lock before sleep. Small beginnings are the ones that survive... and that is the same one-minute rule, coming in its own chapter.

## Sentence of the Week

"If you are not paying the price... then you are the price."

Memorize it just as it is. Every time you download a free app, every time you enter a platform where nobody took a pound from you, every time you watch content for free — remember: someone is paying a price. And the only question you need to keep asking yourself: who is the payer?... And if you can spot no one — then the payer is you.

## Before We Close the Chapter...

So, my friend, you have taken back the first thing that was hidden from you: the bill. And you are now standing on the platform of your mind, watching the trains come and go, knowing who sends them, and able to close the platform in front of any train you do not approve of.

But there is something much deeper than that... and in it the good news and the bad news at the same time.

Even if you locked every notification in the world... there is one voice that will never be locked. A voice that starts working before you open your eyes in the morning, keeps talking to you all day, and changes its words with the mood of the moment: "I am not up to it"... "They will see right through me"... "I am the forgetful type"... "I have always been this way."

Not the voice of the apps. Not the voice of the TV. Not anyone else's voice at all.

It is your own voice — the words you say to yourself. The strongest program your mind has ever run... and the one we monitor the least.
