# The End: A Last Message... and a 90-Day Plan

## The Last Message

If you remember, we began this book from a specific point: you finished the 21-day plan from Part One, and the engine started running... but the car was still standing. And the promise was clear: Part One took back your mind, and this part leads your life.

Now you're here. The last page. And I want to stand with you in it for a moment before you close the book and rise.

First: thank you. Not courtesy — real thanks. A person who reaches the end of a book about leadership and change is someone who passed a silent exam on himself: that he genuinely wants. This book isn't a novel to be beautified in your living room — it's a workshop, and the ones who finish a workshop seriously are few. And you are one of them.

Second: let's agree on one last thing with our usual honesty. This book — with its introduction and its seven chapters and its stories — **isn't what will change your life.** If you return it to the shelf as is, it'll be the most beautiful thing you read this year... and a house of clothes with no one to wear them. The tools inside aren't «ideas» — they're pages: a destination page, a night of decision, a watering page, a return-day plan, a five tally, two hours of Khidmaty. Six pages. What will change your life is this paper and you sitting on it with a pen... and I'm not with you in this moment — you are the one who's with you.

Third — and the most important: I want to tell you that it doesn't matter how much you've arrived from this book. Some people will take from the book one question: «Where am I going?» And some will take all six pages. And both have won. Because of the truth I built the whole book on from the first line: **change isn't a leap — it's a rung.** And any rung starts from here... the point of enough.

And finally: from today, you're not a reader of this book. You're one of its people. And I have one last piece of advice for them... and it's the plan below.

## The 90-Day Plan: The First Quarter of Your Destination's Year

The 21-day plan from Part One had one mission: to start the engine. The 90-day plan's mission is one degree higher: **to lead the car the first 3 months of your destination's year.** 90 days = the first quarter of the 365. If you walk it as is... the rest of the year becomes the same system at a larger degree.

The plan is three phases, thirty days each. And it's built entirely on the book's tools — so it's a quick reference, next to each step is its chapter.

### Phase One: The Foundation (Days 1 → 30)

- **Day 1:** the destination page complete — the three sentences, the choice, the appearance line, the day's rung. (Chapter 1)
- **Day 2:** the first day's rung — the first X mark on the watering page. (Chapter 3)
- **Days 3-7:** daily watering at the exact same rung. No enlarging — enlarging comes later.
- **Day 8:** the night of decision — the standing bill, the change bill, the closed sentence, the signature... and the first bill paid before you sleep. (Chapter 2)
- **Days 9-13:** watering. The voice will come — it doesn't matter: the page has won it.
- **Day 14:** the first expected dry point. The mark of the plan here isn't a failure in it — one minute is enough. (Chapter 2)
- **Days 15-29:** watering. If a day passes: tomorrow two centimetres. (Chapter 3)
- **Day 30:** first-month review: count only the X's. The harvest is forbidden from the count entirely.

### Phase Two: Immunity (Days 31 → 60)

- **Days 31-32:** the five tally — five names, weekly hours, charger/drainer/neutral. (Chapter 5)
- **Day 33:** the one decision for the dose + the chargers list.
- **Days 34-39:** add one charger: a message, a sitting, a group.
- **Day 40:** the return-day plan — three habits and the smallest degree for each, hanging next to the watering page. (Chapter 4)
- **Days 41-59:** watering. And if a storm happens: the smallest degree for the first 7 days... and one added degree from the second week.
- **Day 60:** second-month review: the number of X's + the energy budget — do the sittings charge or drain?

### Phase Three: Impact (Days 61 → 90)

- **Day 61:** if the X's are above 25 in the month: add one rung only. (Chapter 3)
- **Days 62-69:** ordinary watering.
- **Day 70:** the two hours of Khidmaty exercise — your core skill + who needs it around you. (Chapter 6)
- **Day 71:** the first small service. A mark of the other kind on the page.
- **Days 72-89:** two hours of service weekly + sustained daily watering.
- **Day 90:** **the big day** — the first day in your life you're allowed to reckon the harvest. Look at the destination page written on day 1... and look at yourself. That's all. That's all that's in the plan.

### The Four Rules of the Plan (written so they stay hanging)

1. **The order isn't a suggestion.** No jumping steps — each phase builds the one after it.
2. **A day passed? Two centimetres tomorrow.** The plan never gets cancelled.
3. **A week passed? The smallest degree for 7 days.** And after it carry on from where you are — not from the start.
4. **The first 90 days: the count is watering only.** The harvest gets counted from day 90... and before it, it lies.

## And After the 90 Days?

These 90 days aren't a complete plan — they're **the first quarter of your destination's year.** And the thirty-two months after them need from you only two things: the same system... and a slightly bigger rung each quarter. The watering continues, a review every 30 days, a storm happens → the smallest degree, and the service grows by an hour each quarter. And with that you will have lived the year that makes a difference — not the year that passes.

And by the way — this part isn't the last word. On the road is a third part of the series, about the stage after the 90 days: when your destination's first year ends, and the question shifts from «where am I going?» to a bigger question: **«and who will I serve with what I've reached?»** — about leadership: when you move from someone leading his own life... to someone handing the ladder to someone else. If you walk this 90-day plan and find yourself wanting to continue — I'm waiting for you in the same seat.

But before we close... the last sentence in the book. And the whole book was made so you reach it... and rise:

**«I finished the book... doesn't mean I finished.»**

Rise. A page... a pen... and today's rung is waiting for you.

— Mohamed Ismail
