# Appendix B: 12 Ready WhatsApp Messages

These are written to be edited, not copied blindly. Replace the brackets, and keep the order: greeting, information, one question.

## 1. First reply to an inquiry

> Hi [name], the unit is available. [size] sq ft, [n] bedrooms, [finishing]. Price [x], down payment [x]. Want me to send the photos and set up a viewing?

## 2. "Is the price negotiable?"

> It's [x], which is in line with the area. If it's the down payment rather than the price that's the concern, there's a plan I can show you. Want the details?

## 3. "Is there a view?"

> Yes — [floor], facing [open / garden]. Best thing is to see it before sunset, that's when the light is real. Does [day / time] work?

## 4. After a viewing

> Thanks for your time today. The thing you liked most was [feature]. The part that worried you — [objection] — has a solution, and I can explain it here. Shall I start with the price or the down payment?

## 5. The unit isn't right

> Honestly, this one won't give you [what they asked for]. I have [alternative] that's closer to what you described. Want to see that first before spending time on the other one?

## 6. The client went quiet for a week

> Evening [name]. A unit with [new feature] just came up in the same area, and it reminded me of what you said about [priority]. Want me to send it?

## 7. The day before a viewing

> Friendly reminder: tomorrow at [time] in [place]. Message me when you arrive and I'll meet you at the gate.

## 8. Two days after the viewing

> [Name], on your point about [objection]: [short factual answer]. And if you'd like to compare with [alternative], I'm ready.

## 9. The unit sold

> Good news and bad news: the unit you saw was reserved today. I have another one with [feature] in the same project. Want it before it goes?

## 10. Negotiating the price

> Let's do the math properly: my price per sq ft is [x], and the unit you're comparing with is [x] in [condition]. If the gap still doesn't sit right, tell me your number and I'll see what I can do without harming your interest.

## 11. A respectful close

> I don't want to keep pushing. I'll leave you be — and if you need anything in property, even if it isn't mine, I'm here. The moment something matching what you want comes up, I'll let you know.

## 12. After signing

> Congratulations [name]. If you can give me one word about the experience, it would mean a lot. And anything you need afterwards, message me any time.

# Appendix C: 12 Frequently Asked Questions

## 1. Does AI write better than me?

No. It writes faster. That difference matters: your voice is what sets you apart, and the tool buys you the time to show it.

## 2. Do I need to learn to code?

No. You need to describe your job clearly. That is all a good prompt requires.

## 3. Can it invent numbers?

Yes — and it does so confidently. That is why every prompt in this book contains the line "no number that is not in the data".

## 4. Can I put client photos or data into the tool?

Better not. Strip personal data, and work with placeholder names and coded unit numbers.

## 5. Does it understand my local market?

It understands language; it doesn't understand your street. You supply the numbers and context; it supplies the phrasing.

## 6. Which task should I start with?

The listing description. Fastest win, lowest risk, and you can see clearly whether it worked.

## 7. Why are the answers so generic?

Because the prompt is generic. A real prompt has a role, context, constraints, format, and a quality standard.

## 8. Should I let it write the whole ad?

Let it write the draft, then edit the first and last lines yourself. The first line stops the scroll; the last line moves the client.

## 9. Does longer copy sell more?

No. Clarity sells. One line with a number and a feature beats a paragraph of adjectives.

## 10. What do I do when the client says "too expensive"?

Don't discount. Ask one sorting question: market, budget, or value.

## 11. Does it work offline?

Mostly not. So keep your prompts in a file on your own device and use them any time.

## 12. When do I scale up?

After the thirty days, when you're using your prompts without thinking. System first, then expansion.

# Appendix E: A Plain Glossary

| Term | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| **Prompt** | The instructions you give the tool. Output quality equals prompt quality. |
| **Context** | The information you supply about the unit and the client. |
| **Hallucination** | When the tool invents information confidently. The cure: an explicit ban plus verification. |
| **Model** | The version of the tool you're using. It changes over time. |
| **Token** | How text length is measured inside the tool. |
| **Template** | A saved prompt you reuse by changing only the variables. |
| **Automation** | A step that repeats without you touching it. |
| **Agent** | A system that takes several steps on its own — and needs more review, not less. |
| **Backup** | A copy of your prompts and data somewhere else. Not a luxury. |
| **Range** | Saying "between x and y" instead of one confident number. |

## Three more prompts

### Evaluating an ad after 48 hours

> Here is an ad I ran, and here are the results: [views, clicks, messages, duration]. Give me three possible explanations for the result, suggest exactly one change for the next version, and tell me how I'll know whether it worked.

### Analysing client questions

> These are the client questions I received this week: [paste]. Classify them: missing information in the ad, price objection, location objection, ready to view. Then tell me the single most important thing to add to the ad to reduce the first type.

### Building an offer for a torn client

> A client is torn between two units. **Unit A:** [data]. **Unit B:** [data]. **Their priorities:** [paste]. Build a comparison table with only five rows (the ones that matter to them), write one line naming the difference that decides it, and one question I can ask to close the conversation.
