Introduction — Mind Power 4: Who's Driving?

After Part Three came out, I received a message from a reader — the kind that sits in your head for days. He wrote: "I know I've arrived. I know the value of what I reached. I even know I need to benefit others. I did everything the book said. I got my Value Card, walked the Benefit Ladder, and started thinking about the people around me. But every time I try to take a step... something stops me. Not the world. Not people. Me."

"Fear freezes me the moment I need to move. Anger explodes in a moment I needed to stay quiet. Excitement drags me into a decision I knew was wrong. Frustration pins me down on a day I planned to rise. I know the right thing... but I don't do it. As if someone else is driving."

I read the message more than once. Because its author arrived at something few people reach: not just that he knew his value... but that he knew there was a gap. A gap between what he knew and what he did. Between the mind that says the right thing... and what moves on emotion.

This book was written for that gap.

Part Three of "Mind Power" was about the most important shift after arrival: from "What did I reach?" to "What do I gain from what I reached?" A complete ladder: intention → result → value → benefit → leadership → benefiting others. And if you walked the ladder — even with difficulty — you earned something rare: you came to know your value... and began thinking about the value of others.

But right here, there's a battle.

When you knew you needed to lead — lead your life, lead your benefit, lead your giving — the first thing that stands in your way isn't the world. It's you. Not the conscious you... the you that moves on emotion.

And the question this book answers: who's driving?

If the answer every time is: "not me"... then you're the passenger. And the passenger arrives where the driver takes him... not where he wants to go.
